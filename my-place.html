Earthquake today near me: why a 72-hour kit, and how this page works

Public safety agencies in Europe and the United States ask every household to be able to cope alone for at least the first 72 hours of a disaster: after an earthquake, flood or storm, roads, power, phones and shops can be out of reach while rescue teams reach the most urgent cases first. The checklist above is the common core of their guidance: water, food, light, radio, power, medicine, papers, warmth and a plan.

Pick your town and the page reads three open sources straight from your browser: the US Geological Survey for earthquakes of magnitude 2.5 and above, NASA EONET for floods, storms, fires and volcanoes, and the alert feeds of the US National Weather Service and the UN and European Commission GDACS system for official statements. We copy what they publish, with the time, and never rewrite a warning.

ATHENA does not receive your place. It is stored in your own browser and you can remove it with one press. Small earthquakes below magnitude 2.5 and local warnings that no open feed carries are not shown here, so always check your national civil protection and weather service in the list above.

Information only. It is not a replacement for the instructions of civil protection and emergency services. In an emergency call 112 (Europe), 911 (USA) or your local number.
