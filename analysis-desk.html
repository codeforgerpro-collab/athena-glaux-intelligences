How to read an assessment

Six instruments, each named for a watcher from Greek myth. Under each name is what it measures.

  • Scales of ThemisWhat weighs on the risk score
  • Pythia's ladderThe four paths, from the most to the least reported
  • Hermes' routesWhere in the world it is reported
  • Argus watchHow the coverage built up
  • Aletheia's balanceWhether the reporting is one-sided
  • Nemesis lineWhat would prove the reading wrong

Risk assessment

Where the reporting points today, over the horizon you chose. This records what is reported by independent outlets over the last 72 hours; it is not a prediction. Qualitative scale; no percentages.

  • No further escalatory action - Talks and pressure continue
  • Deniable or indirect action - Small or deniable incidents
  • Accidental escalation - Misidentification, miscalculation
  • Overt, attributed action - Strikes or attacks openly claimed

What would change this assessment

This reading is wrong if, before the horizon ends: ATHENA's risk score for this story rises by 2 points or more.

  • Raises risk: New reports from outlets in other countries describing a more serious step.
  • Lowers risk: Reports of talks, a ceasefire or a statement that de-escalates, from more than one origin.

Reports behind this assessment

Where each report comes from. A claim repeated by many outlets is still one claim.

Decision frame

Re-assess if any indicator below appears. For shipping, energy or travel exposure, check insurer, port and government advisories. ATHENA gives analysis, not financial, legal or safety advice.

Scope notices

  • Nuclear dimension: not modelled, so tail risk is understated near any nuclear threshold.
  • Non-state actors: used as context only.

Analysis Desk

What you are looking at

  • A question box and a board of the day's leading geopolitical stories: each dot is a story, higher means more risk, further right means wider coverage.
  • Each assessment shows a summary, a risk assessment, a decision frame, the direction of events and what would prove it wrong.

How to use it

  • Type a question, or select a dot, to open an assessment. Choose a horizon of 14, 30 or 90 days.
  • The method is shown once at the bottom, with the Isonomia scores withheld until the site's data has grown.
  • Each assessment carries six instruments named for watchers from Greek myth, and Kairos lists the week's biggest movers.

Risk colours: gold under 4 · amber 4–6 · orange 6–8 · red 8 and above (scale 0–10).

ATHENA publishes an event only when at least two independent outlets report it.
